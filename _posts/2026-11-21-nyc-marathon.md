On November 1, I ran the TCS New York City Marathon. Bib 60400.

Before that day, the longest I had ever run was a half marathon in the spring, in about 2:20. I went in with a goal of breaking four hours and, honestly, not nearly enough training behind it.

## Getting to the start

{% include photos.html a="/assets/writing/2026-11-nyc-marathon/statue-of-liberty.webp" a_alt="The Statue of Liberty seen from the water on a clear morning" caption="On the water, heading toward the start." %}

Every runner starts on Staten Island. Mile one is the climb up the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge, with thousands of people around you and Brooklyn straight ahead. The overhead sign usually points drivers to JFK. That morning it said "MTA B&T welcomes you to NYC Marathon."

{% include photos.html a="/assets/writing/2026-11-nyc-marathon/verrazzano.webp" a_alt="Runners crossing the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge under a sign welcoming them to the NYC Marathon" caption="The Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge. Mile one." %}

## The city shows up

The course runs through Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx and Manhattan, and people line the streets the whole way just to cheer for strangers. The signs were their own kind of fuel: "WE LOVE HILLS," with a picture of the Queensboro Bridge on it, "You paid money for this?!" and "Where is everyone going?"

{% include photos.html a="/assets/writing/2026-11-nyc-marathon/signs.webp" a_alt="Spectators holding signs that say We Love Hills, You paid money for this, and Where is everyone going" caption="Fair questions, honestly." %}

## The last miles

The final stretch comes down Fifth Avenue and into Central Park, under trees that had just turned for fall.

{% include photos.html a="/assets/writing/2026-11-nyc-marathon/running-1.webp" a_alt="Zayeed smiling while running, wearing bib 60400 and a hydration vest" b="/assets/writing/2026-11-nyc-marathon/running-2.webp" b_alt="Zayeed running among other marathoners under yellow fall trees" caption="Still smiling, somehow." %}

## The finish

By the time I got my medal it was dark. I ended up flat on the ground with the Bangladesh flag over me and the medal on my chest, and that photo says it better than I can.

{% include photos.html a="/assets/writing/2026-11-nyc-marathon/finish-flag.webp" a_alt="Zayeed lying on the street after the race, wrapped in the Bangladesh flag with his finisher medal" caption="Done." %}

My friends were waiting at the end, in the cold, to see me through.

{% include photos.html a="/assets/writing/2026-11-nyc-marathon/finish-friend.webp" a_alt="Zayeed in the orange finisher poncho with his medal, next to a friend" b="/assets/writing/2026-11-nyc-marathon/finish-crew.webp" b_alt="Zayeed with four friends after the race, one of them biting the medal" caption="The crew." %}

26.2 miles, five boroughs, one medal, and a crew that waited for me in the dark.
